Open-source plush robot

Tomo by DreamMachina

Formerly evoKin V.01. A small orange, Mirumi-style bear that hugs a bag strap or handle with spring-loaded arms, and looks around, nods and turns away shyly with a two-servo head. A furry shell around a printed frame, two Feetech FS90MG servos (head pan and tilt), two small torsion springs, and a planned ESP32-S3 brain with a camera and a voice.

How it looks

Blender Cycles renders of the fur model, plus CAD stills of the mechanism. Full-size PNGs are in the repository.

How it works

One egg-shaped body; two springs hold the hug; two metal-gear micro servos move the face cap. The shells are FDM-printed and closed on a registration lip.

Mirumi proportions

Head and body are one egg, about 106 × 104 × 152 mm with fur (Mirumi ≈100 × 118 × 143 mm). The face is a cap cut off by a seam sphere on the tilt axis, so it pans and nods inside the egg. The arm hinges sit at the shoulders, just under the face.

Passive spring hug

Two long, curved, asymmetric arms: the left crosses the chest high and ends low on the far side; the right drops down its flank and sweeps under the belly. Each turns on a vertical M3 pin at the shoulder. A music-wire torsion spring in the knuckle (Grainger 3HPG6 left-hand / 3HPD1 right-hand, 3.36 N·mm per degree) is preloaded 50° against a printed rest stop. You open the arms by hand to a 30° stop and let go. Holding costs zero power.

Diagonal groove and pads

An off-centre channel runs 35° from vertical across the belly, like the line Mirumi's handle takes. It takes round handles 10–15 mm thick, or a flat strap up to 25 mm. TPU sleeves on both arms take the squash; with the arms open the pads are ≈24 mm off the belly, and the handle slides in from the side.

Clamp force

Total pad force about 6.6 N on Ø10, 7.0 N on Ø12.5, 7.3 N on Ø15 and 6.1 N on a flat 25 mm strap (3.2–3.7 N per arm). Assumption: catalogue spring rate, no hinge friction, force = spring torque ÷ the ≈54–57 mm lever to the pad contact. Sitting on a bag, the ≈297 g robot is held upright with 1.4× margin even at 90° lean; hanging free it rolls until its centre of mass is under the handle, with a slip safety factor of ≈2.4 (μ ≈ 0.5 assumed). Not measured.

Head pan and tilt

Two FS90MG servos. Pan: in the yaw collar, ±42° (hard stop ±47°). Tilt: a second servo on the neck turret nods the face cap about an axis near its centre, ±10° (first contact at +16.5° / −23.5°). The tilt servo lead and the camera ribbon cross the pan joint in loose loops.

Firmware

A small ESP32Servo sketch for the XIAO ESP32S3 (firmware/evokin_v01) with three behaviours: look-around, nod and a shy turn-away. No clasp logic — the arms need none. Untested.

Shells and fasteners

Male lip on the back half, female groove on the front, 0.25 mm FDM clearance, 0.40 mm elephant-foot chamfer. M2.5 at 2.8 mm clearance / 2.1 mm pilot. Each arm hangs on an M3 × 30 pin with a washer and nyloc under the knuckle, so an arm and spring come out without opening the torso.

Trade-offs

  • The arms cannot move on their own. No wave, no motorised hug — but no arm current, no gears to strip and no grip interlock.
  • Grip is modest and fixed (≈3.2–3.7 N per arm, 6–7 N total). A stronger spring grips harder but is harder for small hands to open. The spring price is uncertain.
  • The pads are a soft pinch point (≈3.5 N); the arms open by hand.
  • Handle range is Ø10–15 mm round or up to 25 mm flat, loaded from the side. Tomo is meant to sit on the bag like Mirumi; hanging free, it rolls until its centre of mass is under the handle.
  • Taller than Mirumi: ≈152 against ≈143 mm with fur, because the eyes, camera and tilt servo sit at z ≈ 120.
  • Heavier than Mirumi: about 297 g against 155 g, because Tomo carries a speaker and a camera brain.
  • Tilt margin: 6.5° between the +10° clamp and the first contact nodding down.

Electronics

The planned brain is an ESP32-S3, not the Raspberry Pi Zero still drawn as a tray dummy in the CAD. Tomo needs two servo PWM channels (head pan and tilt); the arms need none. Planning notes, GPIO cautions and a bring-up checklist are in electronics/esp32-board-notes.md.

Prototype paths

  • Option A: Seeed XIAO ESP32S3 Sense, plus spare WROOM-1U-N16R8 modules. Pan on D0, tilt on D1.
  • Option B: DFRobot DFR1154. Only GPIO43/44 free, so a PCA9685 servo driver shares I2C with the INA219.
  • Two servos can draw up to 1.6 A at stall; holding a hug draws nothing.

Custom board target

  • ESP32-S3-WROOM-1-N16R8, 65×55 mm, 4-layer.
  • BQ24074, TPS63020, TPS61088 (not MT3608), INA219, TPS259241 eFuse.
  • OV2640/OV3660 on a 24-pin FPC through the neck; INMP441; MAX98357A.
