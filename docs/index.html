Open-source plush robot

Tomo by DreamMachina

Formerly evoKin V.01. A small, long-haired Mirumi-style critter in ivory or pink, about 185 g with its battery, that hugs a bag strap or handle with spring-loaded arms, and looks around, nods and turns away shyly with a two-servo head. A furry shell around a printed frame, two Feetech FS90MG servos (head pan and tilt), two small torsion springs, and a planned ESP32-S3 brain with a camera and a voice.

How it looks

Blender Cycles renders of the fur model, plus CAD stills of the mechanism. Full-size PNGs are in the repository.

How it works

One egg-shaped body; two springs hold the hug; two metal-gear micro servos move the face cap. The shells are FDM-printed and closed on a registration lip.

Mirumi proportions

Head and body are one egg, about 106 × 104 × 152 mm with fur (Mirumi ≈100 × 118 × 143 mm). The face is a cap cut off by a seam sphere on the tilt axis, so it pans and nods inside the egg. The arm hinges sit at the shoulders, just under the face.

Passive spring hug

Two short, thick, symmetric arms, like Mirumi's: each comes round from its own shoulder to the front, where the two mitten paws nearly meet in front of the handle without crossing. Each printed spine sits inside a stuffed fur sleeve (≈Ø26–30 mm over the pile) with a shoulder cap over the knuckle, and turns on a vertical M3 pin at the shoulder. A music-wire torsion spring in the knuckle (Grainger 3HPG6 left-hand / 3HPD1 right-hand, 3.36 N·mm per degree) is preloaded 30° against a printed rest stop. You open the arms by hand to a 60° stop and let go. Holding costs zero power.

Diagonal groove and pads

A shallow channel runs up the centre of the belly, 6° from vertical, so the upright leg of a bag handle sits between the paws like Mirumi's. It takes round handles 10–15 mm thick, or a flat strap up to 25 mm. Fur-coloured TPU pads hidden on the mittens take the squash; with the arms open the pads are ≈29 mm off the belly and the handle comes straight in.

Clamp force

Total pad force about 6.8 N on Ø10, 7.0 N on Ø12.5, 7.3 N on Ø15 and 6.1 N on a flat 25 mm strap (3.2–3.7 N per arm). Assumption: catalogue spring rate, no hinge friction, force = spring torque ÷ the ≈54–57 mm lever to the pad contact. Sitting on a bag, the ≈297 g robot is held upright with 1.4× margin even at 90° lean; hanging free it rolls until its centre of mass is under the handle, with a slip safety factor of ≈2.4 (μ ≈ 0.5 assumed). Not measured.

Head pan and tilt

Two 9 g Feetech FS90 servos (were FS90MG). Pan: in the yaw collar, ±42° (hard stop ±47°). Tilt: a second servo on the neck turret nods the face cap about an axis near its centre, ±10° (first contact at +13.5° / −23.5°). The tilt servo lead and the camera ribbon cross the pan joint in loose loops.

Firmware

A small ESP32Servo sketch for the XIAO ESP32S3 (firmware/evokin_v01) with three behaviours: look-around, nod and a shy turn-away. No clasp logic — the arms need none. Untested.

Shells and fasteners

Male lip on the back half, female groove on the front, 0.25 mm FDM clearance, 0.40 mm elephant-foot chamfer. M2.5 at 2.8 mm clearance / 2.1 mm pilot. Each arm hangs on an M3 × 30 pin with a washer and nyloc under the knuckle, so an arm and spring come out without opening the torso.

Trade-offs

  • The arms cannot move on their own. No wave, no motorised hug — but no arm current, no gears to strip and no grip interlock.
  • Grip is modest and fixed (≈2.8–3.7 N per paw, 5.7–7.4 N total). The preload came down to 30° so the 60° opening stays inside the spring's 90° rating, which it reaches fully open. A stronger spring grips harder but is harder for small hands to open. The spring price is uncertain.
  • The pads are a soft pinch point (≈3 N per paw); the arms open by hand.
  • Handle range is Ø10–15 mm round or up to 25 mm flat, loaded straight in with the handle upright. Tomo is meant to sit on the bag like Mirumi; hanging free from a vertical handle, only friction holds it (slip safety factor ≈3.3, μ 0.5 assumed).
  • Taller than Mirumi: ≈152 against ≈143 mm with fur, because the eyes, camera and tilt servo sit at z ≈ 120.
  • Still heavier than Mirumi: about 185 g with the battery (was ≈334 g) against ≈135–155 g, and 5 g over the 180 g target, because Tomo carries two servos, a pan/tilt neck, a speaker and a camera brain.
  • Short battery life: ≈2 h on 400 mAh (assumed load); plastic-gear servos; a quieter 1 W speaker.
  • Tilt margin: 6.5° between the +10° clamp and the first contact nodding down.

Electronics

The planned brain is an ESP32-S3, not the Raspberry Pi Zero still drawn as a tray dummy in the CAD. Tomo needs two servo PWM channels (head pan and tilt); the arms need none. Planning notes, GPIO cautions and a bring-up checklist are in electronics/esp32-board-notes.md.

Prototype paths

  • Option A: Seeed XIAO ESP32S3 Sense, plus spare WROOM-1U-N16R8 modules. Pan on D0, tilt on D1.
  • Option B: DFRobot DFR1154. Only GPIO43/44 free, so a PCA9685 servo driver shares I2C with the INA219.
  • Two servos can draw up to 1.6 A at stall; holding a hug draws nothing.

Custom board target

  • ESP32-S3-WROOM-1-N16R8, 65×55 mm, 4-layer.
  • BQ24074, TPS63020, TPS61088 (not MT3608), INA219, TPS259241 eFuse.
  • OV3660/OV5640 on a long 24-pin FPC to the nose pod; INMP441; MAX98357A.

Status

  • Prototype. V.01 passive-hug CAD with Mirumi proportions is complete and unprinted. Grip, clinging, spring life and wear are untested.
  • The concept video shows an older design (v3).
  • Nothing has been ordered or purchased. The spring price is uncertain.
  • Power is bench-tethered only. Portable power is unresolved.
  • Not production, not child-ready, not waterproof.
